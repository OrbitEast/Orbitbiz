How to Increase Restaurant Sales

A practical playbook for increasing revenue without relying only on more customers. Focus on average order value, repeat visits, product mix and disciplined weekly review.

1. Make the menu sell, not just list products

Put your strongest-margin and most popular items where customers naturally look first. Use clear descriptions and simple categories. If customers take too long to decide, reduce unnecessary choices and highlight combinations that solve a complete meal.

Action: Identify your top five items by sales and margin. Make them easy to discover and train staff to recommend them naturally.

2. Increase average order value

Instead of pushing customers to buy more randomly, create useful add-ons: drinks, sides, desserts, upgrades or meal combinations. Bundles work best when the value is obvious and the ordering process stays simple.

3. Build repeat business

Record customer preferences where appropriate, maintain consistent service and give customers a reason to return. Rebooking, loyalty offers and timely reminders can be more efficient than constantly finding new customers.

4. Treat delivery as a separate sales channel

Analyse delivery orders by item, time and profitability. Some dishes travel better than others. Improve packaging, photographs, descriptions and combinations while protecting margins after platform and packaging costs.

5. Control stock and waste

Sales growth is weaker when additional revenue disappears through spoilage, over-ordering or stockouts. Review fast-moving ingredients, slow-moving items, waste and purchasing patterns every week.

6. Review the business every week

Track revenue, order count, average order value, gross margin, best-selling items, cancellations, waste and repeat customers. Compare the numbers with the previous week and investigate meaningful changes instead of relying on intuition.

A simple 30-day plan

  1. Week 1: identify best sellers, margins and waste.
  2. Week 2: improve menu placement and create useful bundles.
  3. Week 3: strengthen repeat-customer and delivery workflows.
  4. Week 4: review results and keep only changes that improve the numbers.

Key principle

Restaurant growth is usually a combination of more orders, higher average order value, better repeat business and tighter operational control. Measure each separately so you know what is actually driving improvement.
