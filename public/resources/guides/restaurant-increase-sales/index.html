RESTAURANT BUSINESS GUIDE

How to increase restaurant sales without relying on random discounts.

Restaurant growth is usually a combination of getting discovered, converting visits into larger orders, and giving existing customers a reason to return. The strongest plan improves all three.

1. Make the restaurant easy to discover

Keep your Google Business Profile accurate, including hours, location, phone number, menu information and current photos. Maintain consistent information across your website and ordering platforms.

2. Turn the menu into a sales tool

Group products clearly, highlight signature items, make profitable combinations easy to understand and remove confusing descriptions. A small number of strong recommendations can make decision-making easier.

3. Increase average order value

Use natural add-ons: a drink with a meal, a side with a main dish, a dessert after dinner, or a family bundle for larger groups. Staff should recommend relevant additions rather than pressure customers.

4. Build repeat business

With appropriate permission, create simple reasons to return: weekday specials, seasonal dishes, loyalty rewards, birthday offers or early access to new items.

5. Fix slow periods

Look at sales by day and time. If a period is consistently quiet, design an offer for that specific period instead of discounting the entire week. The goal is to use unused capacity while protecting peak-hour margins.

6. Improve delivery and pickup

Keep delivery menus focused, descriptions clear and packaging suitable for the journey. Identify items that travel well and build bundles around them.

7. Ask for genuine reviews

When customers have a good experience, make it easy for them to leave an honest review. Respond professionally to feedback and correct operational problems that appear repeatedly. Never buy or fabricate reviews.

8. Measure the business every week

Track revenue, order count, average order value, best-selling items, low-selling items, repeat customers, cancellations and waste. Identify one or two changes worth testing next week.

A simple 30-day plan

Week 1: fix local listings, menu information and operating hours. Week 2: improve menu recommendations and create two sensible bundles. Week 3: launch one repeat-customer initiative and one off-peak offer. Week 4: review sales data and keep only the changes that improved the right metrics.
