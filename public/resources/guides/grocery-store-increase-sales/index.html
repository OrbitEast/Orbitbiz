GROCERY RETAIL GUIDE

How to increase grocery store sales and the value of every basket.

Grocery growth is often built from small improvements: fewer stock-outs, better product placement, useful combinations, repeat customers and tighter inventory control.

Keep essential products available

Track fast-moving products and set practical reorder points. Everyday essentials should be managed separately from slower discretionary items.

Build logical combinations

Place products that naturally belong together within easy reach. Useful combinations can make shopping faster while increasing relevant basket items.

Improve shelf visibility

Keep shelves clean, prices readable and high-demand products easy to locate. Rotate stock appropriately.

Manage slow-moving stock

Review products that have not moved for a long period and identify whether price, placement, seasonality, demand or over-purchasing is responsible.

Use simple retention

With appropriate permission, maintain customer information for useful communication about relevant new stock, seasonal products or useful offers.

Make checkout faster

Clear pricing, organised billing and predictable checkout reduce friction and make repeat shopping easier.

Offer local convenience

Accurate online information, clear hours, phone ordering where practical and simple local delivery can make a small store easier to choose.

Review category economics

Compare categories by units sold, revenue, margin, stock value and ageing to identify where working capital should go.

Test one improvement at a time

Move a product group, create a bundle or change a reorder point, then compare results. Controlled tests make results easier to understand.

Weekly checklist

Review stock-outs, ageing inventory, top products, low-selling products, average basket value and repeat-customer activity.
