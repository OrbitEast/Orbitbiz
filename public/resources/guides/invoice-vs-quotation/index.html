Invoice vs quotation: what is the difference?

A quotation and an invoice can contain similar information, but they serve different purposes. Understanding the distinction helps businesses communicate prices clearly, confirm sales correctly and maintain cleaner records.

What is a quotation?

A quotation is a proposal for a product or service at stated prices and terms. It is normally prepared before a customer commits to the purchase. A quotation can describe quantities, prices, taxes, validity dates, delivery terms and other conditions.

What is an invoice?

An invoice is a request for payment for goods or services that have been supplied or are being billed under agreed terms. It records what was sold, the amount due, the invoice date, payment terms and the parties involved.

The key difference

Quotation

Used before the sale is confirmed. It communicates a proposed price and terms.

Invoice

Used when the business is billing for a sale or service. It records the amount payable.

Why the distinction matters

Treating every document as an invoice can make sales records confusing. A quotation may change several times before acceptance, while an invoice should represent an actual billing event. Keeping the stages separate makes customer history, payment tracking and reporting easier to understand.

A practical quotation-to-invoice workflow

  1. Create the customer record.
  2. Prepare a quotation with items, prices and terms.
  3. Revise it if the customer requests changes.
  4. Confirm the sale according to the business process.
  5. Create the invoice from the final agreed information.
  6. Track payment against the invoice.

Common mistakes

Common problems include using the same number series for unrelated document types, changing invoice totals without preserving the underlying sale, forgetting payment terms, and manually re-entering quotation details into an invoice.
