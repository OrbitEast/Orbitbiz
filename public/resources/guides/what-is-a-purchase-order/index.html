GUIDE · PURCHASING

What is a purchase order?

A purchase order, often called a PO, is a business document used to communicate an intended purchase to a supplier. It can provide a structured record of what the buyer wants to order and under which terms.

What does a purchase order contain?

A PO commonly identifies the buyer and supplier, purchase order number, date, requested items, quantities, prices or agreed terms, delivery information and other relevant instructions.

Purchase order vs invoice

Purchase order

Created by the buyer to communicate and track an intended purchase.

Invoice

Created by the seller to bill the buyer for goods or services.

Why businesses use purchase orders

A PO can make purchasing more controlled by recording the request before goods arrive. It gives the business a reference for comparing what was ordered with what was delivered and billed.

From purchase order to stock

  1. Create the supplier record.
  2. Create the purchase order with the required items.
  3. Send or confirm the order according to the business process.
  4. Receive the goods and compare the delivery with the order.
  5. Update inventory from the receiving event.
  6. Record the supplier invoice or purchase transaction.
  7. Track the supplier payment.

Common purchasing problems

Businesses often lose control when orders are placed through scattered messages, received quantities are not checked against the order, or supplier payments are recorded without a clear purchase reference. A connected workflow helps preserve the chain.
