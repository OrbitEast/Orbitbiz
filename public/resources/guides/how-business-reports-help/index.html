GUIDE · REPORTING

How business reports help owners make better decisions

Business reports turn operational records into information that can be reviewed. Their value depends on the quality and consistency of the underlying data.

What should a useful report answer?

A report should help answer a specific business question. Examples include how much has been sold, which invoices remain open, where money is being spent, how inventory is moving or which customer activity needs attention.

Sales reports

Sales reporting can summarise transactions by date, customer, item or other relevant dimensions. The objective is to understand activity rather than simply produce a large table of numbers.

Finance reports

Finance views can help an owner review expenses, payments, receipts and outstanding amounts. Consistent transaction records make these summaries more useful.

Inventory reports

Inventory reporting can show current quantities and stock movement. It can help identify items that need purchasing, unusual adjustments or differences that require investigation.

Customer reports

Customer information can show transaction history, open invoices and recorded activities. This provides context for follow-up and service decisions.

Use reports as a review loop

  1. Capture the underlying transaction accurately.
  2. Review the report for the relevant period.
  3. Identify unusual or important changes.
  4. Investigate the underlying records.
  5. Take an operational action.
  6. Review the next reporting period.

A report is only as reliable as its records

Reports cannot correct missing or duplicated transactions automatically. A connected system can help maintain relationships between customers, sales, invoices, purchases, inventory and finance, but businesses still need sensible processes and review.
