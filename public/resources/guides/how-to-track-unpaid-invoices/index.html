How to track unpaid invoices

Unpaid invoices become difficult to manage when the business relies on memory, scattered messages or separate spreadsheets. A simple status-based workflow can make outstanding amounts easier to review and follow up.

Start with a complete invoice record

Every invoice should have a unique number, customer, issue date, due date, amount and payment status. If these fields are consistent, the business can quickly identify which invoices still require attention.

Separate status from payment history

An invoice can be unpaid, partially paid or paid. Payment history should record the actual receipts or payment events rather than simply replacing the original invoice amount. This preserves the transaction trail.

Review by due date

A practical review can group invoices into upcoming, due and overdue categories. This is more useful than looking only at the total outstanding balance because it shows which customers require immediate attention.

Follow up with context

When contacting a customer, reference the invoice number, original amount, current balance and due date. If a partial payment has already been received, record it before sending the reminder so the customer sees the correct balance.

Record every payment

When payment arrives, record the date, amount and relevant payment account or method. The invoice should then reflect the updated balance. Keeping the payment event linked to the invoice makes later reconciliation easier.

A simple weekly process

  1. Review all open invoices.
  2. Check upcoming and overdue due dates.
  3. Confirm that recent payments are recorded.
  4. Follow up on overdue balances.
  5. Resolve mismatches between invoices and received payments.
  6. Review the outstanding balance in the finance or sales report.

Common mistakes

Typical problems include marking an invoice paid before the payment is actually received, deleting old invoice records, forgetting partial payments and keeping customer reminders outside the main transaction history.

Use connected records

When customer, invoice and payment records are connected, a business can move from a customer profile to the relevant transaction without rebuilding the history manually. OrbitBiz is designed around this connected approach.
