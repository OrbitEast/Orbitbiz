GUIDE · CRM

What is CRM?

CRM stands for customer relationship management. In practical terms, CRM is the process and software a business uses to organise customer information, interactions, follow-ups and relationship history.

What belongs in a customer record?

A customer record can contain contact information, notes, sales history, invoices, payment history and relevant activities. The exact fields depend on the business, but the purpose is consistent: keep useful customer context accessible.

CRM is more than a contact list

A contact list answers “who is this customer?” A CRM workflow can also answer “what happened?”, “what is pending?” and “what should happen next?” This makes follow-up and customer service more systematic.

How CRM connects to sales

When customer records are linked to quotations, sales and invoices, the business can understand the commercial history without searching across unrelated systems. This is especially useful when a customer returns after a previous transaction.

Simple CRM workflow

  1. Create or update the customer record.
  2. Record the relevant interaction or activity.
  3. Prepare a quotation or sale when appropriate.
  4. Link resulting invoices and payments to the customer.
  5. Review open actions and follow-ups.
  6. Use customer history when the next interaction occurs.

Common CRM mistakes

Keeping duplicate customer records, storing important notes only in personal messaging apps, forgetting follow-ups and separating customer information from transaction history can all create avoidable work.

Why connected records matter

A CRM becomes more useful when it connects to the rest of the business workflow. OrbitBiz is designed to connect customers with sales, quotations, invoices and related operational information.
