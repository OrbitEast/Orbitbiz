How to Manage Inventory for a Small Business

Inventory management is the process of knowing what products you have, where they are, how quickly they move and when new stock should be purchased.

Start with accurate item records

Keep consistent names, units, selling information and purchase information for each item. Accurate records make later stock and sales decisions easier.

Set practical stock levels

Identify products that must remain available and products that can be reordered less frequently. Stock levels should reflect demand, supplier lead times and the cost of holding inventory.

Record every stock movement

Purchases, sales, returns, adjustments and transfers can change available stock. Recording these movements helps explain why the current quantity is what it is.

Watch slow-moving stock

Products that remain unsold for long periods tie up working capital and storage space. Review slow-moving items regularly and decide whether to change purchasing, pricing, merchandising or sales priorities.

Connect purchasing and inventory

Inventory decisions are stronger when purchase orders, received goods and supplier records stay connected. This makes it easier to compare what was ordered with what was actually received.

Use connected business records

OrbitBiz connects inventory with sales, purchases, suppliers and reporting workflows so a business can manage stock as part of its wider operations rather than as an isolated list.

Inventory checklist

  • Maintain accurate item records.
  • Record stock movements promptly.
  • Review fast- and slow-moving products.
  • Monitor stock levels before reordering.
  • Compare purchases with received stock.
  • Review inventory reports regularly.
